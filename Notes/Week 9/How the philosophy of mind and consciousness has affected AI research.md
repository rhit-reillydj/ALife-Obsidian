### Brain in a Jar & Cartesian Dualism
- **Thought experiment:** A disembodied brain sustained in a jar raises whether true thinking or consciousness can exist without a body or “thinker.”
- **Descartes’s dualism:** Splits mind (immaterial, seat of consciousness) from body (material automaton), creating the puzzle of how two distinct substances interact.
- **Metaphysical stakes:** If thought requires a subject, then any disembodied AI or “jarred brain” lacks the fundamental unity of thinker and thought.

### Behaviorism & Functionalist AI Foundations
- **Behaviorism (Skinner/Turing):** Equates intelligence with observable behavior—“intelligence is as intelligence does”—but erases internal mental life.
- **Cognitive revolution:** Chomsky’s critique of behaviorism led to functionalism, defining mental states by their causal roles, not by material substrate.
- **Computational functionalism:** Modern AI treats intelligence as information processing modules (vision, language) implemented in computers, regardless of embodiment.

### Searle, Dreyfus, & the Embodiment Critique
- **Chinese Room (Searle):** Symbol manipulation alone—no matter how functionally correct—fails to produce genuine understanding or consciousness.
- **Phenomenology (Dreyfus):** True intelligence relies on bodily skills, context, and lived experience; pure symbol processing ignores the role of the body.
- **Subject vs. function:** Both argue that mimicking functions without an embodied, situated subject leaves “intelligence” hollow.

### Pragmatism in AI & the Metaphysical Gap
- **Pragmatic successes:** AI advances (e.g., vision, NLP) stem from practical shortcuts, not from resolving the metaphysical question of genuine understanding.
- **Persistent gap:** Despite effective tools, AI still lacks unified subjective experience, leaving a fundamental divide between practical utility and true intelligence.

### Ethical Stakes & Requirements for Real AI
- **Mind–body unity:** Any claim to genuine AI must confront the need for sensorimotor embodiment—“legs” for the jar—to ground experience.
- **Moral implications:** Mistaking functional simulations for real minds risks creating unrecognized suffering or denying moral status to conscious beings.
