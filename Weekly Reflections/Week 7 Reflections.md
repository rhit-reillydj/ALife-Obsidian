## What did you learn from the other student presentations on this week's topic?

Will presented **Our Ideology Affects Our Observations and Models**. It had some philisophical ties and was closely related to the ideas of embodiment. An agents interactions with the environment will impact its learning, observations, and future interactions.

Steven presented **Sensorimotor Contingencies**. He explained Kevin O'Regan's work on the topic and gave good examples like a sponge not "generating" softness, the softness comes about from our sensorimotor interaction with the sponge. This could be a beneficial mindset shift in robotics, but it is difficult to grasp in practice.

Alex talked about autopoiesis. Autopoiesis seems to be an attempt at defining what is living. It defines an autopoietic system as one with a clear inside/outside, interactions between the inside/outside, and a low level of randomness.

## How does it connect to what you learned from your own work?

This week, I replicated Karl Sims' "Evolved Virtual Creatures" in 2D instead of 3D. These creatures evolved over time, learning according to their interactions with their environment. They are not autopoietic, but Will and Steven's topics are related to these creatures. As they interact with their environment, they get feedback in the form of a fitness function and grow their understanding of their environment according to these embodied interactions.
