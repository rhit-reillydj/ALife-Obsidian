### What did you learn from the other student presentations on this week's topic?

This week, we learned about different theories of consciousness.

Will presented on Information Theory and Functionalism. Information theory explains consciousness as an organisms ability to process and retain information. The more information that can be processed and stored within the individual, the more conscious they become. Functionalism simplifies consciousness to the functions that arise from it. What is consciousness actually achieving? If any organism were to execute these functions, they would be conscious according to functionalism.

Steven presented on a book that he read some of as well as the book *active inference*. The first book seemed to generally talk about consciousness, what it is capable of, and what is needed for consciousness to arise. *Active Inference* was more interesting to me. It explains the brain as a hierarchical Bayesian inference machine. The brain makes predictions top-down and then corrects for the errors in that prediction from the bottom-up. This is very similar to gradient descent and backpropagation in AI.

Alex presented on emotions and their relationship to consciousness. He explained how different people around the world experience different emotions according to their language, indicating that emotions are learned, not inherent. He then talked about how emotions arise through evolution with emotions that lead to survival and reproduction being passed on to future generations. Emotion is a n-dimensional vector.
